Genetics & DNA

Genetics is the study of genes, heredity and variation in living organisms. Understanding genetics helps us understand how biological information is stored, transmitted and expressed.

What Is Genetics?

Genetics is a branch of biology concerned with how characteristics are passed from parents to offspring and how genetic differences arise among individuals.

Modern genetics is also important in medicine, agriculture, biotechnology, forensic science and biomedical research.

What Is DNA?

DNA, or deoxyribonucleic acid, is the molecule that stores hereditary biological information in humans and many other organisms.

Key idea: DNA contains information that cells use to produce functional molecules, including proteins, and to regulate biological processes.

Genes

A gene is a segment of DNA that contributes to a functional biological product or helps regulate a biological process. Genes are organized within chromosomes.

Chromosomes

Chromosomes are structures containing DNA and associated proteins. In humans, most body cells contain 23 pairs of chromosomes.

DNA Structure

DNA has a double-helix structure. Its sequence is built from four major bases:

  • Adenine (A)
  • Thymine (T)
  • Cytosine (C)
  • Guanine (G)

In double-stranded DNA, adenine pairs with thymine, while cytosine pairs with guanine.

Genetic Variation

Individuals within a population can have differences in their DNA sequences. This genetic variation can arise through processes such as mutation and recombination.

Genetic variation contributes to biological diversity and can influence characteristics such as susceptibility to certain diseases and responses to medicines.

Mutations

A mutation is a change in a DNA sequence. Mutations can have different effects depending on where they occur and how they affect biological function.

Some changes have little or no observable effect, while others can influence biological traits or contribute to disease.

Genetics vs Genomics

Genetics often focuses on individual genes and inheritance, whereas genomics examines the structure, function, evolution and analysis of genomes on a broader scale.

Applications of Genetics in Medicine

Genetics has become increasingly important in modern healthcare and biomedical research.

  • Understanding inherited conditions
  • Studying disease-associated genetic variation
  • Supporting molecular diagnostics
  • Pharmacogenomics and medicine-response research
  • Cancer genomics
  • Population and infectious-disease genomics

Why Genetics Matters

Genetics provides a foundation for understanding life at the molecular level. Advances in sequencing, genomics and bioinformatics continue to expand what researchers can learn from biological information.
