Molecular Diagnostics

Molecular diagnostics uses biological molecules such as DNA and RNA to investigate, identify or monitor biological conditions. It is an important area of modern laboratory medicine and biomedical research.

What Are Molecular Diagnostics?

Molecular diagnostics refers to laboratory approaches that analyse molecular components of biological samples. These approaches can provide information about pathogens, genetic variation and other biological processes.

DNA and RNA Testing

DNA and RNA can contain useful biological information. Depending on the test and the biological question, laboratories may analyse specific genetic sequences to identify or study a target.

Key idea: Molecular diagnostic tests detect or analyse molecular targets rather than relying only on visible characteristics of an organism or disease.

What Is PCR?

Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is a laboratory technique used to amplify specific DNA sequences. Amplification makes a target sequence easier to detect and analyse.

What Is qPCR?

Quantitative PCR, commonly called qPCR, combines DNA amplification with fluorescence-based detection. It allows researchers to monitor amplification during the reaction and obtain quantitative information under appropriate experimental conditions.

Common Applications

  • Detection and investigation of infectious agents
  • Genetic and genomic research
  • Study of inherited conditions
  • Cancer research
  • Research on antimicrobial resistance
  • Biomedical and public-health research

Molecular Diagnostics in Infectious Diseases

Molecular methods can help identify genetic material from infectious organisms. This can be particularly useful when rapid or specific detection is important.

Molecular approaches can also support research into pathogen diversity, transmission and antimicrobial resistance.

Molecular Diagnostics and Genetics

Molecular diagnostic methods can be used to investigate genetic variants associated with inherited conditions or other biological characteristics.

Molecular Diagnostics and Cancer Research

Molecular testing is an important part of cancer research. Researchers can study genetic and molecular changes in tumour cells to improve understanding of cancer biology and investigate potential diagnostic or therapeutic approaches.

Advantages

  • High analytical specificity for suitable targets
  • Ability to detect specific molecular sequences
  • Useful for research and diagnostic applications
  • Can complement other laboratory methods

Limitations

Molecular tests require appropriate laboratory procedures, validated methods, suitable controls and trained personnel. Results also need to be interpreted within the appropriate clinical or research context.

The Future of Molecular Diagnostics

Advances in sequencing, automation, portable technologies, multiplex testing and data analysis continue to expand the possibilities of molecular diagnostics.
