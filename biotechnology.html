Biotechnology

Using living systems, cells and biological molecules to understand problems and develop useful solutions.

What Is Biotechnology?

Biotechnology is the use of biological systems, organisms, cells or biological molecules to develop products, processes and technologies that can benefit society.

Biotechnology brings together disciplines such as biology, chemistry, genetics, microbiology, molecular biology, engineering, computing and medicine.

In simple terms: Biotechnology uses biology to solve real-world problems.

Major Areas of Biotechnology

Biotechnology is a broad field with applications across healthcare, agriculture, industry and environmental science.

  • Medical biotechnology
  • Agricultural biotechnology
  • Industrial biotechnology
  • Environmental biotechnology
  • Marine biotechnology
  • Food biotechnology
  • Computational biotechnology

Medical Biotechnology

Medical biotechnology applies biological knowledge and technologies to healthcare and biomedical research.

Examples include:

  • Molecular diagnostics
  • Genetic testing
  • Vaccines
  • Biological medicines
  • Monoclonal antibodies
  • Cell-based research
  • Gene-based research
  • Cancer research

Medical biotechnology has become increasingly connected with genomics, molecular biology and computational science.

Genetic Engineering

Genetic engineering involves deliberately modifying genetic material using laboratory-based techniques.

Scientists can use genetic engineering to study gene function, produce biological products and investigate potential approaches to disease treatment and prevention.

Modern genetic engineering relies on knowledge of DNA, genes, molecular biology and cellular processes.

Recombinant DNA Technology

Recombinant DNA technology involves combining DNA molecules from different sources to create a new DNA construct.

This technology has played an important role in biotechnology, including research and production of certain biological products.

Recombinant DNA technology is one of the foundations of modern molecular biotechnology.

Biotechnology and Molecular Diagnostics

Biotechnology has contributed to the development of technologies that detect biological molecules associated with diseases or other biological conditions.

Molecular diagnostic approaches can analyse nucleic acids such as DNA or RNA. Techniques including PCR can be used to detect specific genetic sequences when appropriate validated assays are available.

These technologies connect laboratory science with genetics, molecular biology and diagnostic research.

Biotechnology and Genomics

Advances in biotechnology have made it possible to study genomes at increasingly large scales.

Genome sequencing, molecular analysis and computational tools can help researchers investigate genetic variation, disease mechanisms, microorganisms and biological evolution.

This creates a strong connection between biotechnology, genomics and bioinformatics.

Agricultural Biotechnology

Agricultural biotechnology applies biological techniques to agriculture and food production.

Applications can include:

  • Crop improvement
  • Plant tissue culture
  • Marker-assisted breeding
  • Plant disease research
  • Animal biotechnology
  • Improved understanding of agricultural microorganisms

Industrial Biotechnology

Industrial biotechnology uses biological organisms, cells or enzymes to produce useful materials and chemicals.

Microorganisms and enzymes can be used in processes involving areas such as food production, fermentation, bio-based chemicals and other industrial applications.

Environmental Biotechnology

Environmental biotechnology applies biological processes to environmental challenges.

Examples include:

  • Wastewater treatment
  • Bioremediation
  • Biological waste management
  • Environmental monitoring
  • Research involving microorganisms in ecosystems

Biotechnology and Drug Development

Biotechnology contributes to pharmaceutical research by helping scientists understand biological targets and develop biological products.

Modern biotechnology has contributed to areas including recombinant therapeutic proteins, vaccines, antibody-based therapies and other biologically derived medicines.

Drug development is a complex process involving laboratory research, preclinical studies, clinical trials, manufacturing and regulatory evaluation.

Biotechnology in Cancer Research

Biotechnology has become an important part of cancer research. Scientists can study genetic and molecular changes associated with cancer and investigate how these changes influence cells.

Technologies such as DNA sequencing, molecular assays, bioinformatics and cell-based research can contribute to understanding cancer biology.

Cancer research increasingly depends on the integration of molecular biology, genetics, genomics and computational analysis.

Biotechnology and Kenya

Biotechnology can contribute to research and innovation addressing challenges in Kenya and across Africa.

Potential areas include infectious diseases, antimicrobial resistance, agricultural productivity, food security, diagnostics, environmental challenges and biotechnology education.

Building local scientific capacity can help researchers develop solutions that are appropriate for the communities and environments in which they are intended to be used.

Careers in Biotechnology

Biotechnology provides opportunities across laboratory science, research, healthcare, agriculture, industry, education and data science.

  • Medical biotechnology
  • Molecular biology
  • Genomics
  • Bioinformatics
  • Microbiology
  • Biotechnology research
  • Quality assurance
  • Pharmaceutical research
  • Biotechnology education
  • Research data analysis

The Future of Biotechnology

Biotechnology continues to evolve as new technologies become available.

Areas such as genome editing, synthetic biology, artificial intelligence, advanced diagnostics, cell-based technologies and computational biology are expanding the possibilities of biological research.

The future of biotechnology will increasingly involve collaboration between biologists, medical scientists, engineers, computer scientists, researchers and other professionals.
